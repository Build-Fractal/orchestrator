- **Dispute: Invariant 1 Disposition — Outright Reassignment vs. PATCH-Then-Decide**
  - **My claim**: Invariant 1 (single-source versioning) must be reassigned as a named XI-enforcement script for the installer surface, with no PATCH amendment to XI. This is scope-narrowing dressed as clarification, and it sets a precedent that any new principle candidate can effectively shrink an existing principle to make room for itself by proposing a PATCH.

### Final Position Statement

**Non-Negotiables**

1. **CONFORMANCE.md must be supplied and criterion (i) evaluated before any ratification verdict issues.**
   Criterion (i) is BINDING by the cross-tier checklist's own designation, and the arbiter named it "the load-bearing test the blind pass must run." Neither candidate's independence from parent principles can be assessed without the Three-bucket table and membership-basis preamble. A verdict issued before this evaluation is procedurally defective regardless of substantive merit. (Skeptic revision, Rec 3, Surviving; evaluation directive, cross-tier checklist.)

2. **The PENDING/ACTIVE tier requires a named failure consequence — automatic demotion to ADVISORY on missed deadline — not merely a deadline.**
   CLAUDE.md documents exactly what happens when a constitutional-level deadline is set without a consequence: the M033 friendly-tester pass is "overdue past the 2026-05-12 deadline" with no enforcement mechanism. The consequence clause is the structural feature that distinguishes a PENDING/ACTIVE tier from a soft commitment. Ratifying either candidate under a PENDING status without this clause replicates a known failure pattern at the constitutional level. (Skeptic revision, New Rec 2; CLAUDE.md: "friendly-tester pass overdue past the 2026-05-12 deadline.")

3. **Invariant 1's disposition — reassignment or survival — must be resolved before Candidate A ratification, and any PATCH amendment to XI must be independently motivated.**
   A ratification proceeding while Invariant 1's relationship to XI remains unresolved produces a constitutional record that is either duplicative (if XI already covers the surface) or unjustifiably narrow (if XI was patched solely to make room). Both outcomes are worse than resolving the question before ratification. The test for whether a PATCH to XI is legitimate is whether its rationale can be written without reference to Candidate A. If it cannot, reassignment is the only sound path. (Skeptic revision, Rec 1, Surviving; constitution XI: "Duplication across files is a consistency bug waiting to happen.")

**Flexibility**

1. **The Invariant 2 Principle X evaluation should complete before Candidate A ratification, but I accept that the outcome determines structure — not necessarily existence.**
   What I must preserve: the evaluation must be a ratification input, completed before the arbiter's verdict. What I am flexible on: if the evaluation finds Invariant 2 constitutionally distinct from X (because a bill-of-materials declaration differs structurally from a behavioral policy declaration in X's sense), Candidate A proceeds with Invariant 2 as its sole surviving content. The inflexible requirement is that the finding be recorded before the verdict, not that the finding must be a specific conclusion.

2. **The reader-exception table in Candidate B must contain a verbatim pattern, but I am flexible on its precise format and location.**
   What I must preserve: an exception entry that asserts a dynamic reader exists without recording its actual access pattern is invalid and treated as absent. The verbatim-pattern requirement is the mechanism that prevents the escape hatch from becoming an assertion-only bypass. What I am flexible on: whether the exception table lives in CONFORMANCE.md or a co-located sidecar, the exact column structure of the table, and the precise wording of the reader definition sub-clauses (a/b/c), provided the substance — canonical-key consumption via static or recorded-dynamic access — is unambiguous.

3. **The Invariant 1 PATCH-then-decide path is acceptable if and only if the scope restriction is independently motivated — I am flexible on which resolution the synthesizer chooses, provided the independence test is explicit in the record.**
   What I must preserve: the ratification record must state explicitly whether the XI scope restriction (if issued) was independently motivated or motivated solely by Candidate A's admission, and if the latter, must explain why that does not constitute a circular admission argument. What I am flexible on: if the arbiter finds a credible independently-motivated rationale for restricting XI's scope (e.g., documented author intent at XI's ratification time that "state, configuration, and knowledge" referred to runtime orchestrator data specifically), I accept the PATCH path. The inflexible requirement is that the independence test be explicit in the record — not implicit in the rationale.
