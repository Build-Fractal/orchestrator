### Systemic Contradictions

- **Scope boundary as an implicit admission mechanism**
  - **Manifests in**: Contradiction U1 (XI vs. Invariant 1), Contradiction R2 (VIII vs. Candidate B), Phase 1 skeptic dispute D-A3 (X mislabeling and Invariant 2), Phase 1 advocate missed opportunity on Invariant 2 X overlap.
  - **Root cause**: The candidates' distinctness arguments assert that existing principles' scope excludes the candidates' targets, but these scope restrictions are stated in the proposal text rather than in the existing principles' normative bodies. This means each candidate's admission depends on a reading of parent principles that the parent principles do not textually support. Both candidates attempt to carve space from parent principles (XI, VIII, X) by asserting restrictions those principles do not record.
  - **Implication for spec**: Distinctness arguments in constitutional proposals must cite textual authority from the parent principle's normative body, not from the proposal's own framing of that body's scope. Where no such textual authority exists, the correct path is a PATCH amendment to the parent principle that establishes the boundary with documented rationale — and the independence test (the rationale can be written without referencing the new candidate) must be explicit in the amendment record. This is a constitutional authoring discipline, not merely an editorial preference.

- **Normative scope exceeds demonstrated verifier coverage**
  - **Manifests in**: S5 (Candidate B scope vs. one demonstrated verifier class), S6 (Invariant 2 Principle X evaluation), A2 (reader definition gap), A1 (stub-to-live gate), the PENDING/ACTIVE convergence.
  - **Root cause**: Both candidates make normative claims that exceed what their named verifiers can mechanically enforce at ratification time. Candidate A names three verifier stubs; Candidate B names one verifier covering one of four claimed artifact classes. A principle whose normative scope outruns its verifier coverage produces a constitutional mandate that is partially enforceable and partially dependent on human judgment — precisely the failure Principle II was designed to prevent.
  - **Implication for spec**: Constitutional proposals should be scoped to verifier coverage at ratification time, with an explicit extension clause as the mechanism for scope expansion. The PENDING/ACTIVE tier handles verifier maturity within a fixed scope; the extension clause handles scope expansion. These are complementary mechanisms addressing different lifecycle phases, and both should be present in the ratified text from the first ratification event.

- **Structural classification of quality gates vs. constitutional invariants**
  - **Manifests in**: Contradiction R1 (Invariant 3), Contradiction R4 (threshold debate), S2, A7, the Quality Gates section of the constitution as a pre-existing structural home.
  - **Root cause**: Candidate A conflates release-procedure requirements (install testing at every version-tag publication) with architectural mandates (the three-invariant structure). The constitution has distinct structural homes — constitutional principles for architectural mandates, Quality Gates for release evidence — but the candidate places a release-evidence requirement inside the principle body. This conflation blurs the amendment pathway: relaxing a constitutional invariant requires a MINOR or MAJOR version bump; updating a Quality Gates entry does not.
  - **Implication for spec**: Future candidate principles should pass a "governance-home test" before ratification: is this a permanent architectural mandate, or a release-procedure requirement? The former belongs in the principle body; the latter belongs in Quality Gates. The test question is: would relaxing this requirement in a future release require constitutional deliberation, or would it be operational maintenance? If the answer is operational maintenance, it belongs in Quality Gates.

- **Ratification timing vs. prerequisite completeness**
  - **Manifests in**: Contradiction R4 (adopt-and-gate vs. reform-before-admitting), A-N1 (XI PATCH or reassignment), A-N2 (VIII PATCH prerequisite), S3 (CONFORMANCE.md procedural block), the converged PENDING/ACTIVE mechanism.
  - **Root cause**: The deliberation process assumes ratification is binary (PASS/FAIL), but both candidates present scenarios where partial ratification is logical — some invariants may be constitutional, others are not; one candidate's scope is ratifiable at a narrow level, not at the claimed broad level. The constitution's Governance section describes amendment types (MAJOR/MINOR/PATCH) but does not address staged ratification of multi-invariant proposals.
  - **Implication for spec**: Multi-invariant constitutional proposals should require independent distinctness evaluation per invariant before ratification, with the final verdict addressing each invariant separately. A proposal with three invariants may ratify one, reassign one to an existing principle's enforcement notes, and move one to Quality Gates in a single ratification record — without requiring the whole proposal to either pass or fail as a unit.

- **Cross-principle enforcement surface ambiguity**
  - **Manifests in**: R2 (VIII vs. Candidate B verifier surface split), A8/S-N1 (joint scope table necessity), S4 (VIII PATCH mandate), advocate cross-review of skeptic "Dangerous Contradictions — Candidate B and Principle VIII."
  - **Root cause**: When a new principle shares a scope boundary with an existing principle, both principles' enforcement surfaces (run-doctor.sh vs. check-dead-infra.sh) become ambiguous without a single authoritative boundary declaration. A maintainer applying both principles to the same artifact cannot determine from the principle texts which verifier is authoritative. The constitution's Principle XI (Single Source of Truth) applies to the enforcement layer itself — two verifiers covering potentially overlapping surfaces without a declared hierarchy violates XI.
  - **Implication for spec**: Any new principle that explicitly shares a scope boundary with an existing principle should be required to declare that boundary in a canonical location (CONFORMANCE.md or an equivalent) as a condition of ratification, not a post-ratification editorial improvement. This requirement should be documented in the constitution's Governance section as a mandatory ratification artifact.
