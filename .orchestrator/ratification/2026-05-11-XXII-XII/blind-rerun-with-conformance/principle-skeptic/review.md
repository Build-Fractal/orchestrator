### Executive Summary

The spec presents two candidate constitutional principles — Candidate A (Distribution Surface Integrity) and Candidate B (No Dead Infrastructure, config-knob class) — for blind evaluation against the grounding constitution's Inclusion Criteria: mechanical verification capability, falsifiable scope, and distinctness from existing principles I–XV. The evaluation is grounded in a 15-principle constitution that explicitly inherits Inclusion Criteria from a parent namespace, applied here as a structural gate, not a recommendation.

Both candidates address legitimate governance gaps. Candidate B is the stronger of the two: its linter (`check-dead-infra.sh`) exists, has been validated against the canonical scope, and a quantified baseline has been established before ratification. Candidate A is more ambitious but names three scripts as mechanical verifiers while acknowledging each is "currently a path-existence stub" — a claim that conflates feasibility with enforcement. More critically, Candidate A's Invariant 1 (single-source versioning) is substantively indistinguishable from existing Principle XI (Single Source of Truth) on the plain text of both, and Invariant 2 (force-include discipline via manifest.txt) recapitulates the pattern Principle X (Templating Over Inference) already governs. Neither overlap is automatically fatal if scope boundaries are declared explicitly, but neither is declared explicitly in the candidate text — the distinctness section addresses only XI and ignores X entirely.

For Candidate B, the principal failure is precondition, not form: Principle VIII's normative body already names "configuration entry" as subject to its reachability requirement, and declaring a new principle for config-knob liveness without first amending VIII to exclude that sub-scope installs an overlap that future auditors must resolve by reading deliberation artifacts rather than the constitution itself. The scope boundary is real, but it requires an VIII PATCH to become load-bearing.

**Most important recommendation**: Candidate A must either fold Invariant 1 into a scope-bounded amendment to Principle XI, or supply a definition-grounded argument explaining why installer-embedded version strings are not "configuration" under XI — the current label distinction ("STATE/CONFIG/KNOWLEDGE" vs. "DISTRIBUTION SURFACES") is not a structural argument and will not survive a competent distinctness challenge.

This is structurally correct. The Inclusion Criteria require mechanical enforcement feasibility; naming a script with a described behavior — even a stub — satisfies the *form* of the criterion. The grounding constitution's Principle II requires that "must-haves be checkable without human judgment" [`constitution.md`, § II]. Both candidates at minimum establish the correct structural commitment to this requirement.

- **Candidate B quantified baseline** (Candidate B, "Baseline 2026-05-11: 0 dead leaves across 41 leaves"): Providing a measured baseline before ratification is strong practice aligned with Principle II's requirement that evidence precede claims [`constitution.md`, § II]. The baseline demonstrates the principle is not speculative — it governs a surface already enumerated and audited. This is the correct model for new-principle evidence.

- **Candidate B explicit scope boundary** (Candidate B, "Scope boundary" section): Candidate B explicitly distinguishes its config-knob class from Principle VIII's file-system scope and from Principle XIV's speculative-complexity prohibition, with a rationale for each. Declaring scope boundaries in the candidate text is the correct structural move for a principle neighboring existing ones. The boundaries are discoverable from the candidate itself without reading external deliberation material.

- **Falsifiable violation examples** (Candidate A "Falsifiable scope"; Candidate B "Falsifiable scope"): Both candidates supply concrete hypothetical PRs with specific files, field names, and expected verifier outputs. This is the exact form Principle II's "verification is a mechanical gate, not an LLM compliance exercise" clause demands [`constitution.md`, § II]. A reader can determine pass/fail for each hypothetical without interpretation.

### Missed Opportunities

- **Invariant 1 distinctness argument from Principle XI is a label swap, not a structural argument**: Candidate A states it is "distinct from existing principle XI (Single Source of Truth)" because XI governs "STATE / CONFIG / KNOWLEDGE" while Candidate A governs "DISTRIBUTION SURFACES." This does not establish structural distinctness. Principle XI's normative body says "every piece of orchestrator state, *configuration*, and knowledge MUST have exactly one authoritative location" [`constitution.md`, § XI, emphasis added]. Version strings in installer scripts are configuration. The candidate does not argue that installer-embedded version strings fall outside XI's definition of "configuration" — it only uses a different noun. Without a definition-grounded argument, Invariant 1 is redundant with XI on plain text. **Impact: high.**

- **Candidate A makes no distinctness argument against Principle X**: Candidate A's distinctness section names only Principle XI. It makes no reference to Principle X (Templating Over Inference), which states "Configuration and policy MUST be declared in templates... not inferred by scripts at runtime. Scripts implement mechanics; templates declare policy" [`constitution.md`, § X]. A `manifest.txt` is a declarative artifact that governs what ships in a bundle — this is precisely the X pattern applied to the packaging domain. Invariant 2's force-include discipline either folds into an X amendment or requires an explicit argument for why manifest governance differs from the template-over-inference pattern X already establishes. The absence of any X-distinctness argument is a gap the candidate must address before inclusion. **Impact: high.**

- **Path-existence stubs do not discharge mechanical verification capability**: Candidate A describes three verifiers ("currently a path-existence stub") under a "Mechanical verification" heading. A path-existence stub tests whether a file exists at a given path — not whether the invariant the file is meant to enforce holds in the current codebase. The Inclusion Criteria require that "a reader can determine pass/fail mechanically." A stub that emits PASS when its path exists emits PASS regardless of whether a new installer has a hardcoded version string, which is the exact violation Invariant 1 is meant to catch. The spec should explicitly distinguish feasibility (a working enforcement script can be written and placed at this path) from current capability (a working enforcement script exists at this path and does enforce the invariant). As written, the heading implies the latter while the parenthetical admits only the former. **Impact: high.**

- **Candidate B requires an VIII PATCH as a precondition for a load-bearing scope boundary**: Candidate B's scope boundary — "file-system-level infrastructure reachability of helper scripts and reference doc files is governed by existing constitution principle VIII" — is a sensible decomposition. But Principle VIII's body says "every file, script, template, and *configuration entry* MUST be reachable from a live code path" [`constitution.md`, § VIII, emphasis added]. Config knobs in `templates/orchestrator-config-default.yml` are configuration entries. The scope boundary Candidate B claims is not derivable from VIII's current text; it requires VIII to be amended to specify that "configuration entry" means config-file reachability as a file-system artifact, not variable-level liveness of individual knobs within it. Without the VIII PATCH, a future reader of VIII can reasonably conclude VIII already covers Candidate B's domain. **Impact: medium.**

- **Invariant 3 is the strongest distinctness anchor but is buried**: Invariant 3 (end-to-end install testing at every release gate) has no clear analog in Principle XI (version SST) or Principle X (declarative templates). It governs a genuinely distinct concern: whether the assembled distribution surface works end-to-end, not merely whether it is versioned or manifested correctly. But Candidate A bundles Invariant 3 with Invariants 1–2 under a single principle header, so its distinctness argument is obscured by the weaker arguments for Invariants 1 and 2. The inclusion case for Candidate A would be significantly stronger if it led with Invariant 3 and positioned Invariants 1–2 as enabling constraints (you need a stable version to test; you need a complete manifest to test the right files) rather than independent invariants of equal weight. **Impact: medium.**

- **Invariant 3 lacks definitions required for mechanical falsifiability**: Invariant 3 requires "every release gate runs each per-runtime installer against a fresh project fixture and verifies the project's status command works." Neither "fresh project fixture" nor "works" is defined. Without these definitions, installer-smoke.sh cannot emit a deterministic PASS/FAIL — a human must judge whether the output constitutes "working." This is precisely the human-judgment gate Principle II prohibits in mechanical verification [`constitution.md`, § II]. The minimum definitions needed: what directory state constitutes "fresh," and what exit code and output pattern constitutes "works." **Impact: medium.**

- **PENDING clause-mapping cells in the three-bucket scaffold are not excluded from Candidate B's normative body**: Candidate B's normative body includes "SKILL.md-equivalent config option declared in command frontmatter" as in-scope. The clause-mapping scaffold (CONFORMANCE.md, "Tier 2 XII — Clause-mapping scaffold") labels the corresponding row "PENDING — scope not yet drawn (frontmatter-shaped vs body-shaped)." A principle whose declared scope includes surfaces with PENDING verification paths cannot claim full mechanical verification feasibility for those surfaces. The candidate should either exclude frontmatter-shaped config options from its current normative body or mark them explicitly as deferred extensions pending clause-mapping. **Impact: low.**

### Off-Base Assumptions

- **"Mechanical verification" conflated with "path-existence" in Candidate A**: Candidate A's three verifier descriptions each close with "currently a path-existence stub" but appear under a "Mechanical verification" heading. The spec assumes that naming a script path with a described enforcement behavior establishes mechanical verification capability. It does not. Mechanical verification capability requires that the described behavior, when implemented, produces a deterministic pass/fail based on codebase state — not that a file placeholder exists. The distinction matters for Inclusion Criteria evaluation: feasibility (the enforcement is implementable) and current capability (the enforcement is implemented and running) are different claims. The spec treats them as equivalent and should not.

- **Scope boundary between XI and Candidate A assumed self-evident from domain labels**: Candidate A assumes the boundary between XI's "STATE / CONFIG / KNOWLEDGE" domain and Candidate A's "DISTRIBUTION SURFACES" domain is obvious enough to substitute for a structural argument. It is not. Installer scripts read configuration files and embed configuration values (version strings) into their logic. A future maintainer reading XI's text — "every piece of orchestrator configuration MUST have exactly one authoritative location" — would reasonably conclude XI already covers installer-embedded version strings, because those strings are orchestrator configuration. The boundary exists as a reasonable policy choice but requires explicit declaration, not assumption.

- **Candidate B assumes Principle VIII's "configuration entry" is read as file-level, not knob-level**: Candidate B's scope boundary rests on reading "every... configuration entry MUST be reachable from a live code path" [`constitution.md`, § VIII] as meaning file-system reachability of the config file as a whole, not variable-level liveness of individual entries within it. This reading is defensible but not the only plausible reading — "configuration entry" can mean an individual config key. The spec treats this reading as established when it is not: the scope boundary requires an VIII PATCH to become load-bearing on the plain text of the constitution as it currently exists.

### Disputes

**Dispute 1 — Candidate A Invariant 1 fails the distinctness criterion against Principle XI** (BLOCK if unresolved before inclusion)

The distinctness argument offered is "XI governs STATE / CONFIG / KNOWLEDGE; Candidate A governs DISTRIBUTION SURFACES." This is a label distinction, not a structural argument. Principle XI's normative body covers "every piece of orchestrator state, configuration, and knowledge" [`constitution.md`, § XI]. Version strings in installer scripts satisfy the plain-text definition of "configuration." The candidate must do one of three things before inclusion: (a) argue from XI's definition that installer-embedded version strings are not "configuration" under XI — with a citation to that definition; (b) propose an XI scope boundary amendment that explicitly excludes distribution-surface configuration from XI's coverage; or (c) fold Invariant 1 into an XI amendment that extends XI to explicitly cover distribution-surface version strings. Without one of these three moves, Invariant 1 duplicates XI on plain text and fails the distinctness criterion.

**Dispute 2 — Candidate A makes no distinctness argument against Principle X** (FLAG)

Candidate A's distinctness section addresses Principle XI only. It makes no argument for distinctness from Principle X (Templating Over Inference). Principle X states: "Configuration and policy MUST be declared in templates... not inferred by scripts at runtime" [`constitution.md`, § X]. A manifest.txt enforcing force-include discipline is a declarative artifact that governs what ships in a bundle — structurally, this is the X pattern applied to the packaging domain. If Invariant 2 is a domain-specific application of X, it either folds into an X amendment or requires an explicit distinctness argument explaining why bundle manifests differ from the template-over-inference governance X already covers. The absence of any X-distinctness argument is a gap the candidate must close before inclusion.

**Dispute 3 — Candidate A's path-existence stubs do not satisfy mechanical verification capability** (FLAG)

The Inclusion Criteria require mechanical verification capability: a reader can determine pass/fail mechanically without human judgment. Candidate A describes three verifiers as "currently a path-existence stub." A stub that tests its own path existence cannot determine pass/fail for the invariant it is meant to enforce — a new installer with a hardcoded version string would not be caught by a stub that only checks whether `scripts/verify/version-source-of-truth.sh` exists. The spec should acknowledge this as a feasibility claim, not a capability claim: "Path existence demonstrates that enforcement is implementable at this path; working enforcement is deferred to post-ratification implementation." The Inclusion Criterion for mechanical verification is met at the feasibility level; the current framing overstates it.

**Dispute 4 — Candidate B requires an explicit VIII PATCH as a prerequisite for a load-bearing scope boundary** (FLAG)

Candidate B declares a scope boundary against Principle VIII by asserting that VIII governs "file-system-level infrastructure reachability" while Candidate B governs "variable-level liveness within configurable infrastructure artifacts." This boundary is sensible and consistent with the deliberation record. However, Principle VIII's text says "every file, script, template, and configuration entry MUST be reachable from a live code path" [`constitution.md`, § VIII]. The phrase "configuration entry" is not glossed as "config file as file-system artifact" in VIII's current text — it could be read as individual config knobs. The scope boundary Candidate B claims is not derivable from VIII's plain text alone; it requires VIII to be amended to specify that "configuration entry" in VIII's context means file-system reachability of the config file, not variable-level liveness of individual keys within it. Candidate B should either include the VIII PATCH text as a prerequisite or acknowledge explicitly that inclusion is blocked pending the PATCH.

**Criterion (i) — Implicit relief detection (BINDING): PASS**

The CONFORMANCE.md three-paragraph independence argument (Tier 2 Inheritance Basis, Paragraphs 1–3) does not grant implicit relief from any parent-constitution requirement without routing through the formal Relief pathway. Paragraph 1 explicitly acknowledges that all three SOURCE B Purpose-clause prerequisites — (a) multi-agent deliberation substrate, (b) plugin entry-point boundary shape, (c) free/paid partition — are absent in orchestrator. Paragraph 2 constructs scope-independence arguments grounded in the normative body of each inherited principle: XXII's three invariants "govern the domain of installable artifact distribution and apply to any project that ships installable artifacts"; XII's normative body "governs the domain of configurable infrastructure registration and applies to any project that registers configurable infrastructure via templates or config files." These are structural arguments about the domain each principle's normative body governs, not role-claims ("we are the implementing tool for X"). Paragraph 3 disposes each prerequisite by arguing the inherited principle's normative body has no dependency on that prerequisite — for each of the three, the argument names a specific invariant and explains why it operates independently of the missing architectural feature. Criterion (a): the argument is not silent about any parent prerequisite that the inherited principle's normative body depends on — all three are explicitly acknowledged. Criterion (b): no role-substitution is present — the argument grounds independence in the principle's normative body, not in the project's function. Criterion (c): no scope-narrowing of the inherited principle is claimed; the argument is that the principle's existing scope includes orchestrator's domain. Criterion (i) PASS.

**Criterion (ii) — Scope-boundary discoverability (advisory): FLAG**

Two scope boundaries in CONFORMANCE.md require reading external documents to interpret:

1. The "next MAJOR" trigger for the Tier 2 tracking policy references "SOURCE B Amendment Process, L1181" — this line is not reproduced in CONFORMANCE.md and is not discoverable from CONFORMANCE.md alone. These are advisory findings only; they do not block the headline verdict. However, they create maintenance risk when SOURCE B is amended and CONFORMANCE.md must be updated by a maintainer who has not read the original deliberation tree.

**Criterion (iii) — Verification-evidence sufficiency (advisory): FLAG**

The three-bucket XII structure (CONFORMANCE.md, "Tier 2 XII — Three-bucket structure") labels one bucket "Satisfied" with evidence: "`scripts/diagnostics/check-dead-infra.sh` + `tests/test-dead-infra-knobs.sh` against `templates/orchestrator-config-default.yml` (41 leaves; baseline 0 dead)." This distinction is implicit in the "Provisional" label; it would benefit from an explicit "Criterion 1 feasibility only; Criterion 3 enforcement deferred" notation in each stub row so future auditors can interpret the status without traversing the deliberation tree. Advisory flag, not blocking.

### Actionable Recommendations

   - **Risk if ignored**: A subsequent compliance reviewer applies both XI and Invariant 1 to the same artifact class, creating a dual-principle audit burden for a single governance concern.
