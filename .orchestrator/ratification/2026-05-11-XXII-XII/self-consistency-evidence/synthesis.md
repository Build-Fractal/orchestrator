### Systemic Contradictions

- **Pre-ratification evidence pattern**
  - **Manifests in**: The L175-185 delete-vs.-cite contradiction (Rec 1 coherence vs. pairwise Phase 1 Alignment §3); the analytical-baseline contradiction (coherence's Dangerous Contradictions §3 vs. pairwise's entire Phase 1 scope analysis); the XII design-before-code inversion (coherence Rec 5 vs. pairwise Alignment §2).
  - **Root cause**: The amendment's supporting artifacts (CONFORMANCE.md rows, linter scripts, pending constitutional note) were written assuming a favorable ratification outcome before any deliberation occurred. This conflates "evidence prepared for ratification" (CONFORMANCE.md rows, enforcement scripts — legitimately pre-ratification) with "artifacts produced by ratification" (constitutional amendments, version bumps — must wait for deliberation closure).
  - **Implication for spec**: The proposal should explicitly distinguish these two categories. Evidence artifacts (CONFORMANCE.md Component-tier declarations, `check-dead-infra.sh`) may precede ratification and serve as the deliberation's evidentiary basis. Ratification artifacts (Principle VIII amendment text, version line at L355, CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md closed entry) must not appear in the authoritative documents until after the third deliberation closes with a PASS verdict. Any future proposal structured around inheritance declarations should declare this boundary explicitly in the ratification-path section.

- **Multi-document scope authority fragmentation**
  - **Manifests in**: The CONFORMANCE.md pointer tension (coherence Rec 4 targeting the Governance section vs. pairwise Rec 7 targeting the proposal text); the "invisible from constitution alone" diagnosis (coherence Missed Opportunities §3); the "proposal assumes readers cross-reference CONFORMANCE.md independently" assumption (pairwise Off-Base Assumptions §2); the installed-artifact reachability gap (coherence New Rec 2 / pairwise Rec 4 dispute).
  - **Root cause**: The inheritance pattern (Tier 2 → component-tier) is new infrastructure. There is no established canonical routing rule for where inherited-principle scope boundaries are registered. CONFORMANCE.md does this work but is not named as the registry in the Governance section, in the proposal, or (until this amendment) in the constitution itself.
  - **Implication for spec**: CONFORMANCE.md must be established as the single canonical registration point for inherited-principle scope boundaries, with two navigational pointers: a Governance-section pointer in the constitution (durable, for future amendment authors) and a proposal-level citation (immediate, for this deliberation set). Both the Governance-section pointer (coherence Rec 4) and the proposal citation (pairwise Rec 7) are necessary; neither is sufficient alone. Future inheritance declarations that lack a CONFORMANCE.md row must be blocked by the Governance section's pointer as an amendment requirement.

- **Scope-precision vs. process-validity sequencing conflict**
  - **Manifests in**: The two-phase implementation sequencing rule (pairwise New Rec 2); the L175-185 removal as Phase 1 prerequisite (coherence Rec 1); the "version bump before scope text" requirement (coherence Rec 2); the Phase 2 dependency framing dispute (pairwise Dispute 3 vs. coherence "contingent" language).
  - **Root cause**: The proposal's scope-precision text was written with L175-185 as its evidentiary basis. After L175-185 is removed, that basis must be replaced with CONFORMANCE.md. Drafting scope-precision text before the baseline is corrected produces ratification artifacts that reference pending text as settled evidence. The two types of P1 recommendations (process-validity vs. scope-precision) are not peers; they have a strict sequencing constraint.
  - **Implication for spec**: Constitutional amendment proposals that depend on pending text (text that will become authoritative only upon the amendment's own ratification) must declare their Phase 1 / Phase 2 structure explicitly. Phase 1 corrections establish the authoritative baseline; Phase 2 substantive additions are drafted against that baseline. No ratification proposal should be structured so that its scope-precision claims trace through its own pending outcome.

- **Single-principle ratification pattern vs. batched-inheritance analysis gap**
  - **Manifests in**: The single-deliberation-set justification tension (pairwise Rec 3 / Rec 5 vs. coherence's dismissal of the Change 5/XII tension as non-existent; pairwise Missed Opportunities §5); the scope-separation analysis absence that forced both agents to derive it independently across multiple phases.
  - **Root cause**: The three-deliberation pattern was designed for single-principle ratification. Batching XXII + XII together is a new pattern with no corresponding requirement for a cross-principle scope-separation analysis before the deliberation begins. The proposal justified the batching on adoption-feasibility grounds (same inheritance shape, same evidence posture) without addressing the interaction question that batching creates.
  - **Implication for spec**: Any ratification that batches multiple inheritance declarations must include an explicit pairwise interaction paragraph in the proposal — before the deliberation set begins — specifying how the inherited principles apply to dual-governed artifacts. The paragraph must answer: which principle is load-bearing when both apply to the same artifact? The interaction analysis is the proposal author's responsibility, not the deliberation's. This requirement should be added to the ratification-path description as a mandatory section for batched declarations.

- **Enforcement boundary predates governance boundary**
  - **Manifests in**: The XII design-before-code inversion (coherence Rec 5); the CONFORMANCE.md three-bucket structure emerging as a side effect of setting up the inheritance row rather than from constitutional deliberation; the installed-artifact reachability gap that was not covered by any principle's enforcement scope because it emerged from the packaging layer that was built after the principles were written.
  - **Root cause**: The Tier C alignment sweep ported enforcement infrastructure (`check-dead-infra.sh`, XXII verification scripts) before declaring the constitutional inheritance, inverting Principle III. As a result, the governance boundary (what does Principle VIII cover vs. inherited conversus Tier 2 XII?) was determined by examining the existing enforcement scripts rather than by deliberating the constitutional question first.
  - **Implication for spec**: Future alignment sweeps that ship enforcement infrastructure before declaring inheritance should document two artifacts at implementation time: (1) a CONFORMANCE.md row that declares the scope boundary at the artifact-class level, and (2) an explicit note in the CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md (or its predecessor log) identifying the implementation as a discovery-phase spike whose constitutional ratification is pending. This would make the design-before-code inversion visible and bounded at the point of occurrence rather than requiring reconstruction during the ratification deliberation.
